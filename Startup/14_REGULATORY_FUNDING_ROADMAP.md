## PART A — THE REGULATORY PATH (CDSCO / MDR 2017)

India regulates medical devices under the **Medical Device Rules 2017 (MDR 2017)**, administered by **CDSCO**. Risk classes:

| Class | Risk | Examples | Who approves |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **A** | Low | thermometer, stethoscope | State licensing authority |
| **B** | Low-moderate | BP monitor, **most measurement/screening wearables ← YOU** | State licensing authority |
| **C** | Moderate-high | ventilator, infusion pump | Central (CDSCO) |
| **D** | High | implants, life-support | Central (CDSCO) |

**Your Device #1 (Class B) needs, roughly:**
1. **ISO 13485 QMS** in place (your quality system) — the foundation of everything.
2. **ISO 14971 risk management file** for the device (this is partly what *sets* your class).
3. **IEC 60601** electrical-safety + EMC testing at an accredited lab (a powered patient-contact device must pass).
4. **IEC 62304** software lifecycle documentation (your firmware is regulated software).
5. **ISO 10993** biocompatibility for skin-contact parts (electrodes/straps).
6. **CDSCO manufacturing licence (MD-5 for Class A/B)** via the state authority, with a **Notified Body audit** of your QMS.
7. Clinical *evidence* — for Class B this is often a **clinical evaluation / performance study**, generally lighter than the full human clinical *trials* that Class C/D demand. (Confirm the exact evidence bar for your specific device with a consultant — it varies.)

**Rough cost & time to a certified Class-B device (ballpark — verify!):**
- QMS (ISO 13485) setup + certification: **~₹2–5L/yr**
- IEC 60601 safety + EMC testing: **~₹5–20L** per device (lab-dependent)
- Biocompatibility testing: **~₹1–5L**
- Consultant + CDSCO licence + documentation: **~₹2–8L**
- **Total idea → certified Class-B market: realistically ₹40L–1Cr+ and ~3–5 years.**

> This is exactly why **certification money is NOT family money.** It comes from grants + investors (Part B). Your family funds only the ≤₹1–3L Rung-0 learning phase.

**Hard eligibility fact you must plan around (verified Jul 2026):**
> **BIRAC BIG requires an employed applicant to undertake to QUIT their job and go full-time if selected**, and to be **incubated at an incubator.** → With your bond running to **~late 2027 – early 2028** (nominal Jul 21 2027, extended ~3× by leave days taken), **BIG is off the table until it ends.** This isn't a blocker; it *sets the sequence*: garage/skills during the bond → grants after. (Silver lining: a later bond-end buys you *more* Phase-0 skill-building runway before the quit-job clock starts.)

**Hyderabad is arguably India's best city for this** — use it:
- **T-Works** — public hardware prototyping facility (pay-per-use, no residency; your Rung-0 workshop)
- **IKP Knowledge Park**, **AIC-CCMB**, **T-Hub** — life-science / deep-tech incubators (your BIG/PRAYAS host)
- **IIT-Hyderabad CfHE** (healthcare-entrepreneurship fellowship — full-time, so *post-bond* only)
- **AMTZ Vizag** — India's dedicated medtech manufacturing zone, one train away (for the manufacturing phase)
- Tailwind: India imports ~70–80% of medical devices; govt actively funds **import-substitution** — a domestic novel device is politically and financially favoured.

**Incubator vs solo garage — your question, answered:**

| | Solo garage (Phase 0, during bond) | Incubator (Phase 2+, post-bond) |
|:--|:--|:--|
| **Best for** | Learning, prototyping, problem discovery | Money, clinical access, mentors, credibility |
| **Pros** | Free, flexible, no dilution, fits job+bond | Grants, ISO/regulatory mentors, hospital intros, lab equipment, investor pipeline |
| **Cons** | No clinical access, no mentors, isolation (medtech punishes solo) | Competitive entry, milestone reporting, some take 2–8% equity, BIG needs you full-time |
| **Time commitment** | Yours entirely | T-Works & pre-incubation are flexible/virtual; only full-time fellowships clash with a job (skip those until post-bond) |

**Verdict: it's a sequence, not a choice.** Garage now (you must — bond + no fundable project yet). Incubator the moment the bond ends and you have a validated problem + prototype. And honest truth: **you would NOT be accepted today** — no specific problem, no prototype, no clinical partner. Phase 0–1 exist precisely to make you fundable by ~2028.

## PART D — THE HONEST PASS-PERCENTAGES (gate by gate)

No single misleading top-line number. Each gate, estimated honestly (same method as the Germany roadmap's per-university odds):

| Gate | Milestone | Pass odds | What moves it |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **G0** | Finish Rung-0 prototypes (become a real biosignal engineer) | **80–90%** | Just consistency; you already have the base skills |
| **G1** | Lock a validated, well-scored problem (P1 exit gate) | **60–70%** | Clinical access is the risk; Hyderabad hospitals help |
| **G2** | Working, credible prototype of Device #1 | **55–65%** | Hardware is hard; solvable with time + T-Works |
| **G3** | Get into an incubator + win PRAYAS (~₹10L) | **40–55%** | Prototype + clear need makes this very winnable |
| **G4** | Win BIRAC BIG (~₹50L) | **25–40%** | Heavily oversubscribed; most first attempts fail — reapply |
| **G5** | Achieve ISO 13485 + IEC 60601 + CDSCO Class-B licence | **45–60%** | Money-gated more than skill-gated; slow but tractable |
| **G6** | Clinical validation + first real sales of Device #1 | **35–50%** | Selling a device is 10× slower than selling software |
| **G7** | Ladder to a Class C/D neuro device | **30–45%** | Only reachable *because* G6 built the credibility |

**Cumulative, honestly:**

| Outcome | Rough probability |
|:--|:--|
| You become a genuinely skilled biomedical-device engineer (G0) | **~85%** — nearly certain if you stay consistent |
| You lock a real problem + build a credible prototype (G2) | **~40–50%** |
| Device #1 gets grant-funded and certified (through G5) | **~15–25%** |
| Device #1 actually sells / a real company exists (G6) | **~8–15%** |
| You reach the Class C/D neuro dream (G7) | **~3–8%** |

> **Read these the right way.** The low top-line (a certified, selling device is single-digit-to-teens likely) is *normal for hardware medtech* — it's a hard, multi-year climb. **But your downside is excellent:** even if you stall at G2, you emerge a skilled medtech embedded engineer with prototypes — exactly the profile for a **Germany embedded-biomedical masters** (Bremerhaven, `00`) or a top medtech job. Unlike the v2 software path, **every rung you climb has real salvage value.** That asymmetry — modest odds of the dream, excellent odds of a strong fallback — is what makes going all-in *rational*, not reckless.
