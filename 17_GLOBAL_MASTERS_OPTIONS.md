### Two routes into medical embedded work in Germany
- **Route A — Embedded master's** (Bremerhaven, Dortmund, Chemnitz) **+ medical-device thesis + medtech Werkstudent job.** Strongest *embedded* depth.
- **Route B — Biomedical Engineering master's** (Lübeck, HAW Hamburg) **+ embedded thesis.** Stronger *medical* depth; you'd still do electronics/instrumentation, and you'd write your thesis on device firmware.

Both work. Route B puts you physically next to Dräger or Philips, which matters a lot for the thesis and job.

# PART 2 — ITALY

### Why Italy is a serious backup
- **Tuition is low** (€2,500–3,900/year at the top public universities).
- **DSU regional scholarships** are open to **all nationalities**: tuition refund **+ a cash grant of roughly €2,850–8,300/year**, often with free meals and sometimes housing. Eligibility depends on family income *and assets* (the "ISEE parificato" — thresholds roughly €25,000–28,000).
- **Mirandola (Emilia-Romagna)** is Europe's largest biomedical-device district (dialysis, transfusion and infusion devices).

### Programs

| University | Program | Lang. | Fee/year (non-EU) | Fit | Notes |
|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|:---|
| **University of Pisa + Scuola Sant'Anna** | **M.Sc. Embedded Computing Systems** | English | **≈ €2,530** | ⭐⭐⭐ | Almost exactly your field. ⚠️ **Only 20 seats for non-EU students** and a **compulsory competitive entrance exam**. English B1+. |
| **Politecnico di Milano** | **Laurea Magistrale Biomedical Engineering** | English | €900–3,900 by family income; **non-EU residing abroad are placed in the top band (~€3,900)** | ⭐⭐⭐ | Top-ranked engineering school. Admission evaluates your bachelor's grades and background — competitive. Lombardy is also a strong medtech region. |
| Politecnico di Torino, Univ. of Padova, Bologna, Genova | Electronic / Biomedical / Bioengineering (several in English) | English | ~€2,500–4,000 | ⭐⭐ | Worth a second research pass if Italy becomes a real option. |

### Italy — the catches
- **Salaries are lower than Germany** (entry engineering roughly €28–35k vs €45–55k in Germany), and **many jobs expect Italian**.
- **DSU and your family's assets:** the ISEE counts **property**. Your house may push you over the threshold. Ask an Italian CAF/DSU office for an estimate before counting on the grant.
- **Process:** "pre-enrolment" on the **Universitaly** portal + a **CIMEA statement or Dichiarazione di Valore** (Italy's version of degree verification) + visa. Deadlines are often **Jan–Apr for a September start**.
- **Backlogs:** Italian admissions look at your grades and transcript; they don't generally publish backlog rules. **Ask directly**, same as Germany.

5. **Politecnico di Milano — Biomedical Engineering**
6. Check your **DSU eligibility** once, with the house counted.

### One Asian "lottery ticket" that costs only effort
7. **MEXT (Japan) Embassy scholarship** — deadline ≈ **1 April 2027**. It's fully funded; one attempt costs you an application and an exam day. Check whether 7.5/10 clears the Embassy's GPA bar first.

### Skip
UK, USA, Canada, Australia, Switzerland, Norway, Sweden, Netherlands, Singapore, KAUST — either 2–4× your budget or out of reach with 15 backlogs.

### The thing that matters more than the country
Every option above — Germany, Italy, Ireland, Taiwan, Japan — gets **easier with the same three things**: **IELTS 7.0**, **a GitHub with real embedded + biosignal projects**, and **honest, well-explained backlogs**. Those are your actual levers. The country list is just where you point them.
